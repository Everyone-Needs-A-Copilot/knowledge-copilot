# Personal-layer baseline

The individual layer. It shapes how every copilot **writes** (knowledge/chat) *and* **works**
(CLI/coding) for you. It's the same idea whether you're solo or inside a large enterprise, and
it lives in your **personal tier** (`<C>-copilot-private`) — never in the company layer.

The wizard scaffolds these as stubs for you to fill:

| Doc | Why it adds value |
|---|---|
| `writing-style.md` | Tone, rhythm, formatting — steers prose *and* commit messages, PR text, docstrings |
| `working-style.md` | Async/direct, detail depth — sets verbosity, proactiveness, explain-vs-just-act |
| `decision-principles.md` | Your heuristics & tradeoff priorities — lets a copilot make defensible calls unprompted |
| `communication.md` | Brevity, when to ask vs. proceed, report format — governs how a copilot escalates |
| `expertise-map.md` | Deep vs. light areas — the copilot stops over-explaining your strengths, flags weak spots |
| `bio-positioning.md` | Who you are / how you frame your work — anchors first-person writing and READMEs |
| `values.md` | What you optimize for and won't trade — a tiebreaker for ambiguous requests |
| `red-lines.md` | Hard prohibitions (words, tactics, libraries, licenses, data practices) — never crossed |
| `stack-defaults.md` | Preferred languages, frameworks, formats — the copilot reaches for your defaults |

Keep each short and first-person. This is the highest-leverage content you can give a copilot,
and it costs nothing to start — one paragraph per doc beats none.
