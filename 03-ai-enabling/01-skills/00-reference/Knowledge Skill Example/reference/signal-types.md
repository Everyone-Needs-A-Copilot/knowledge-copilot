# Signal Types Catalog

Detailed catalog of competitive signal types with examples across different contexts.

## Advantage Signals (Toward Losing Ground)

Signals that give the competitor a specific, current edge.

| Subcategory | Description | Product Example | Account Example |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Feature gap | A capability you don't have | "Their platform ships native mobile support" | "They demoed a report our tool can't produce" |
| Price | A materially lower entry cost | "Their starter tier is 30% cheaper" | "Procurement flagged the price difference" |
| Integration | A connection you don't support | "They integrate natively with the prospect's stack" | "Our integration requires a workaround" |
| Brand trust | Category leadership or recognition | "They're the default choice in this vertical" | "Their logo carries more weight in this board" |
| Speed | Faster time-to-value | "Their onboarding is measured in days, not weeks" | "The prospect wants to be live before quarter-end" |

## Momentum Signals (Toward Losing Ground)

Signals that the competitor is actively gaining traction.

| Subcategory | Description | Product Example | Account Example |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Funding | Recent investment fueling growth | "They raised a large round and are spending on ads" | "Their sales team just doubled" |
| Win rate | Recently winning deals like this one | "They've won 3 deals in our pipeline this quarter" | "Two peer companies switched to them recently" |
| Hiring | Investment signals from job postings | "They're hiring aggressively in this product area" | "They just hired a head of enterprise sales" |
| Press | Positive public coverage | "They were featured in a major industry roundup" | "The buyer mentioned reading about them" |
| Community | Growing user or advocate base | "Their user community is large and vocal" | "The champion has friends who use them" |

## Weakness Signals (Toward Holding Ground)

Signals that reveal a real gap or vulnerability in the competitor's offering.

| Subcategory | Description | Product Example | Account Example |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Support quality | Worse service or response times | "Their support is community-forum-only" | "Reference call cited slow support tickets" |
| Reliability | Outages or quality complaints | "Review sites cite frequent downtime" | "The prospect had a bad experience with them before" |
| Onboarding | Slower or harder implementation | "Their onboarding takes 2x as long" | "A mutual contact called their setup painful" |
| Scope | Missing a capability the buyer needs | "They lack the reporting depth this segment needs" | "The buyer called out a specific missing feature" |
| Pricing model | Confusing or punitive pricing | "Their usage-based pricing surprises customers" | "The prospect was burned by a surprise invoice" |

## Switching Cost Signals (Toward Holding Ground)

Signals that create friction against moving to the competitor.

| Subcategory | Description | Product Example | Account Example |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Data migration | Effort to move historical data | "Migrating 3 years of data would take weeks" | "They'd lose their whole audit trail" |
| Integrations | Existing connections that would break | "They have 12 integrations wired to our platform" | "Their finance workflow depends on our exports" |
| Training | Team familiarity with the current tool | "Their team is fully trained on our workflows" | "Retraining the team is a real cost they've flagged" |
| Contract terms | Existing commitments or lock-in | "They're mid-term on an annual contract" | "Early termination has a real financial penalty" |
| Relationship | Trust built with your team | "Their champion has worked with us for years" | "They explicitly said they trust our team" |

## Signal Interaction Patterns

Signals don't exist in isolation. Common interaction patterns:

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Amplifying** | Two signals reinforce each other | High advantage (price) + strong momentum (funding) = real threat |
| **Canceling** | Opposing signals neutralize | Strong advantage + equal switching cost = stalemate |
| **Cascading** | One signal triggers another | Closing the top weakness gap reveals a hidden advantage signal |
| **Seasonal** | Signals vary by timing | Budget pressure peaks at renewal; feature gaps matter most at evaluation |
| **Asymmetric** | One side dominates | Multiple weak advantages can't overcome one intense switching cost |
