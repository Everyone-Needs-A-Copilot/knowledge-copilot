# Time Management

Here is the uncomfortable truth about time management: you cannot manage time. Time does not care about your to-do list. It moves at the same pace whether you are in a flow state or scrolling your phone. What you can manage is your **energy**, your **attention**, and your **decisions** about what gets those limited resources.

Most productivity advice focuses on fitting more into the day. This skill takes the opposite approach: do fewer things, but the right things, at the right time, with full attention. A focused 90-minute deep work session produces more than a scattered 8-hour day of context switching. The goal is not to be busy. The goal is to be effective -- and then to stop, because rest is not the opposite of productivity, it is the foundation of it.

## Framework

### Energy Management Over Time Management

Your energy fluctuates predictably throughout the day. Match your work to your energy, not the clock.

| Energy State | Characteristics | Best Used For | Worst Used For |
|-------------|----------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Peak** | Highest focus, sharpest thinking, most willpower | Deep work, creative work, hard problems, strategic thinking | Email, meetings, admin |
| **Sustain** | Good energy, decent focus, moderate willpower | Collaborative work, writing, planning, moderate-complexity tasks | The hardest intellectual work |
| **Recover** | Lower focus, decision fatigue setting in | Admin, email, routine tasks, organizing | Creative work, important decisions |
| **Rest** | Energy depleted, focus gone | Actual rest, light reading, walks, socializing | Anything requiring quality output |

### Chronotype-Aware Scheduling

| If You Are... | Peak Window | Sustain Window | Recover Window |
|--------------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Early bird** | 6am - 10am | 10am - 2pm | 2pm - 5pm |
| **Standard** | 9am - 12pm | 1pm - 4pm | 4pm - 6pm |
| **Night owl** | 10am - 1pm (or 8pm-12am) | 2pm - 5pm | Morning (before peak) |

**How to find your chronotype:** Track your energy levels every 2 hours for a week. Rate focus and motivation 1-10. Patterns emerge fast.

### Deep Work Protocol

Deep work is focused, distraction-free effort on cognitively demanding tasks. It is where your most valuable output comes from.

| Element | Protocol |
|---------|----------|
| **Environment** | Designated workspace. Phone in another room. Notifications off. |
| **Duration** | 60-120 minutes per block. Most people max out at 4 hours of deep work per day. |
| **Ritual** | Same start routine each time (coffee, close tabs, open only what you need, start timer) |
| **Single task** | One project, one problem, one piece of work. Not three. |
| **Buffer before and after** | 10-15 minutes to transition in and out. Do not schedule meetings right after. |
| **Shutdown** | Clear end. Close the work, write a quick note on where you left off, walk away. |

### Task Batching Framework

Context switching is the silent killer of productivity. Every switch costs you 10-25 minutes of refocusing time.

| Batch Category | Examples | Ideal Frequency |
|---------------|---------|----------------|
| **Communication** | Email, Slack, messages, phone calls | 2-3 designated windows per day |
| **Administrative** | Expense reports, scheduling, forms, updates | One block per day or per week |
| **Creative** | Writing, design, strategy, problem-solving | 1-2 deep work blocks per day |
| **Meetings** | Calls, one-on-ones, group meetings | Cluster on 2-3 days, keep other days clear |
| **Errands** | Shopping, appointments, pickups, drop-offs | One errand run per week when possible |
| **Maintenance** | Home tasks, organizing, cleaning | Designated time block, not scattered throughout the day |

### The Maker vs. Manager Schedule

| Schedule Type | Structure | Best For |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| **Maker schedule** | Large uninterrupted blocks (3-4 hours). A single meeting in the middle ruins the day. | Creating: writing, coding, designing, building |
| **Manager schedule** | Hour-long blocks. Meetings and decisions fill the day. Switching is the norm. | Coordinating: managing people, making decisions, unblocking others |
| **Hybrid** | Maker mornings (deep work, no meetings before noon). Manager afternoons (meetings, email, collaboration). | Most knowledge workers |

## Application

### Time Audit Methodology

Before optimizing, understand where your time actually goes. Perception and reality rarely match.

| Step | Action | Duration |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Track everything** for 5 business days. Every 30 minutes, note what you did. | One week |
| 2 | **Categorize** each block: Create, Coordinate, Consume, Admin, Recovery, Waste | 30 min |
| 3 | **Calculate percentages** -- Where did time actually go? | 15 min |
| 4 | **Compare to ideal** -- Where would you like time to go? | 15 min |
| 5 | **Identify the gap** -- What needs to change? What can be eliminated, batched, or moved? | 30 min |

#### Time Audit Categories

| Category | Examples | Target % for Makers | Target % for Managers |
|----------|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Create** | Deep work, writing, building, designing | 40-50% | 15-25% |
| **Coordinate** | Meetings, emails, Slack, calls | 15-20% | 35-45% |
| **Consume** | Reading, learning, research | 10-15% | 10-15% |
| **Admin** | Scheduling, expenses, forms, organizing | 5-10% | 10-15% |
| **Recovery** | Breaks, walks, lunch, social | 10-15% | 10-15% |
| **Waste** | Unintentional scrolling, unfocused time | <5% | <5% |

### Weekly Rhythm Design

Design your ideal week with recurring blocks:

| Time | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday |
|------|--------|---------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Morning** | Deep work | Deep work | Meetings | Deep work | Review/Plan |
| **Midday** | Lunch + walk | Lunch + walk | Lunch + walk | Lunch + walk | Lunch + walk |
| **Afternoon** | Meetings | Admin batch | Deep work | Meetings | Wrap-up + prep |
| **Evening** | Personal | Personal | Personal | Personal | Personal |

**Themed days** (optional, for those with diverse responsibilities):

| Day | Theme | Focus |
|-----|-------|-------|
| Monday | Strategy | Planning, big-picture thinking, prioritization |
| Tuesday | Create | Deep work on primary projects |
| Wednesday | Connect | Meetings, collaboration, one-on-ones |
| Thursday | Create | Deep work on secondary projects |
| Friday | Review | Wrap up loose ends, plan next week, learning |

### Protecting White Space

White space is unscheduled time. It is not wasted -- it is where thinking, recharging, and spontaneity happen.

| Protection Strategy | How to Implement |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Block "unavailable" time** | Put blocks on your calendar even if they are for yourself |
| **Meeting-free mornings** | Decline or reschedule morning meetings by default |
| **Buffer between meetings** | Never schedule back-to-back. 15-minute minimum gap. |
| **One meeting-free day per week** | Guard it fiercely. This is your most productive day. |
| **Lunch is not free time** | Eat, walk, rest. It is not an open slot for meetings. |
| **End-of-day shutdown** | Hard stop at a defined time. Work will fill whatever container you give it. |

### The Two-Minute Rule (David Allen)

If a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately. If it takes more than two minutes, schedule it.

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Quick email reply | Do it now |
| Filing a document | Do it now |
| Scheduling a meeting | Do it now (or batch with other scheduling) |
| Writing a report | Schedule a deep work block |
| Researching a topic | Schedule a focused research session |
| "Let me think about that" | Schedule thinking time (yes, literally) |

## Quick Reference

### Daily Non-Negotiables

- [ ] One deep work block (60-120 min) during peak energy
- [ ] Communication batched into 2-3 windows (not constant monitoring)
- [ ] At least one real break (walk, rest, not phone scrolling)
- [ ] End-of-day shutdown: write tomorrow's top 3 priorities, close everything

### Weekly Planning Ritual (30 min, Sunday or Friday)

- [ ] Review what got done this week
- [ ] Identify the 3 most important outcomes for next week
- [ ] Schedule deep work blocks for those outcomes
- [ ] Review calendar for conflicts and buffer gaps
- [ ] Batch administrative tasks into one or two blocks
- [ ] Ensure at least one meeting-free half-day exists
- [ ] Check: is there white space? If not, what can move?

### Energy Management Cheat Sheet

| Feeling | What It Means | What to Do |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| Sharp, focused, motivated | Peak energy | Start your hardest, most important work immediately |
| Productive but not brilliant | Sustain energy | Collaborative work, writing, moderate tasks |
| Foggy, restless, checking phone | Recovery zone | Admin tasks, email, routine work |
| Exhausted, making mistakes | Depleted | Stop working. Walk, rest, eat. Pushing through produces garbage. |

### The "Should I Do This Now?" Filter

```
Is this urgent AND important?
  YES --> Do it now or in the next available block.

Is this important but NOT urgent?
  YES --> Schedule it in a deep work block this week.

Is this urgent but NOT important?
  YES --> Can someone else do it? Delegate.
         If not, batch it with other admin.

Is this neither urgent nor important?
  YES --> Decline, delete, or ignore.
```

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|--------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Checking email first thing in the morning | Gives your peak energy to other people's priorities | Deep work first, email after |
| Back-to-back meetings all day | No time to think, process, or create | Cluster meetings, protect maker time |
| "I work best under pressure" | You work fastest, not best. Quality suffers, stress compounds. | Start earlier with focused blocks |
| Multitasking | Your brain cannot do two cognitive tasks simultaneously. It switches, poorly. | Single-task during deep work. Batch similar tasks. |
| No planned breaks | Decision fatigue, diminishing returns, burnout | Schedule breaks as firmly as meetings |
| Open-ended work sessions | Work expands to fill the time given (Parkinson's Law) | Time-box everything. Deadlines create focus. |
| Saying yes to everything | Your calendar fills with other people's priorities | Default to no. Protect your time for what matters most. |
| Optimizing the wrong things | Perfecting your to-do system instead of doing the work | The best system is the one you actually use. Keep it simple. |


## Quality Tests

### Practicality Test
- Plan fits within actual available time and resources
- **PASS:** Total time commitment estimated and fits available schedule
- **FAIL:** Plan assumes unlimited time or resources

### Completeness Test
- Plan covers the full scope of the task or system
- **PASS:** All steps from start to finish are enumerated
- **FAIL:** Key steps missing or assumed to be obvious
