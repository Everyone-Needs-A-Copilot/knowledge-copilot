# Interview Guide Design

## Overview

Create interview guides that go beyond surface-level questions to uncover the forces driving decisions. Guides balance structure (ensuring coverage) with flexibility (following the conversation). Grounded in Jobs-to-Be-Done methodology.


## Define Done

- [ ] Analysis inputs gathered from relevant data sources
- [ ] Framework/methodology applied systematically (not ad hoc)
- [ ] Findings supported by specific evidence, not assumptions
- [ ] Actionable recommendations produced with clear next steps

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define Research Objectives

Before writing questions, clarify what you need to learn:

| Element | Question to Answer |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Core question** | What is the one thing we must understand? |
| **Decision context** | What decision will this research inform? |
| **Knowledge gaps** | What do we think we know vs. need to verify? |
| **Participant profile** | Who has the experience we need to understand? |

```markdown
## Research Brief
**Core question:** [Single sentence]
**Decision this informs:** [What changes based on findings]
**Current assumptions:** [What we believe but haven't validated]
**Ideal participant:** [Who has done the thing we're studying]
```

### Step 2: Structure the Guide

Use this proven flow structure:

| Section | Purpose | Time |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Opening** | Build rapport, set context | 5 min |
| **Background** | Understand participant's context | 10 min |
| **Timeline** | Walk through specific experience | 20 min |
| **Forces exploration** | Dig into push/pull/anxiety/habit | 15 min |
| **Reflection** | Abstract learnings, future needs | 5 min |
| **Close** | Thank, next steps, referrals | 5 min |

### Step 3: Write Opening Questions

The opening sets the tone. Keep it conversational:

```markdown
## Opening (5 minutes)

1. Before we dive in, tell me a bit about yourself and your role.
   - [Warm-up, builds rapport]

2. I'm curious about [topic]. There are no right or wrong answers — I'm just trying to understand your experience.
   - [Sets expectations, reduces performance anxiety]
```

### Step 4: Write Timeline Questions

The timeline is the core. Focus on a specific, recent experience:

```markdown
## Timeline: The Story (20 minutes)

**Anchor to specific event:**
1. Think back to when you [did the thing]. Can you walk me through what happened?

**First thought:**
2. What first made you think about [change/purchase/action]?
   - How long had that been on your mind?

**Trigger event:**
3. Was there a specific moment when you decided to actually do something about it?
   - What was happening that day?

**Consideration:**
4. What options did you consider?
   - How did you find out about them?
   - What made you rule some out?

**Decision:**
5. Walk me through the actual moment of decision.
   - Where were you? Who was involved?
   - What tipped you over?

**First use:**
6. What was the first [day/week/time] like?
   - What surprised you?
```

### Step 5: Write Forces Questions

Explicitly probe the Four Forces of Progress:

```markdown
## Forces Exploration (15 minutes)

**Push (what was wrong with the old way):**
- What was frustrating about how things were before?
- What would have happened if you'd done nothing?

**Pull (attraction to new solution):**
- What excited you about [new thing]?
- What did you hope would be different?

**Anxiety (fears about changing):**
- What concerns did you have before switching?
- What almost stopped you?

**Habit (comfort with status quo):**
- What did you like about the old way?
- What do you miss about how things were?
```

### Step 6: Write Probing Questions

Prepare probes for depth:

| Probe Type | Example |
|------------|---------|
| **Clarification** | "What do you mean by [term they used]?" |
| **Example** | "Can you give me an example of that?" |
| **Emotion** | "How did that make you feel?" |
| **Contrast** | "How is that different from [alternative]?" |
| **Magnitude** | "On a scale of 1-10, how much did that matter?" |
| **Silence** | [Wait 5 seconds after they finish] |

### Step 7: Write Closing Questions

End with reflection and logistics:

```markdown
## Closing (5 minutes)

1. Looking back, what's the biggest thing that's changed for you?

2. If you could go back and give yourself advice, what would you say?

3. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?

4. Do you know anyone else who has gone through something similar?
   - [Referral for recruitment]

Thank you so much for your time. This has been incredibly helpful.
```

## Examples

**Output: Complete Interview Guide**

```markdown
# Interview Guide: [Project Name]

## Research Brief
**Core question:** Why do small business owners switch accounting software?
**Decision this informs:** Feature prioritization for migration tool
**Current assumptions:** Price is main driver, data migration is main barrier
**Ideal participant:** SMB owner who switched accounting software in last 6 months

## Interview Flow

### Opening (5 min)
1. Tell me about your business — what do you do, how long have you been running it?
2. Who handles the books? You, a bookkeeper, accountant?

### Background (10 min)
3. How did you first start tracking your business finances?
4. What software were you using before the switch?
   - How long had you been using it?
   - How did you originally choose it?

### Timeline (20 min)
5. Take me back to when you first started thinking about switching. What was happening?
6. Was there a specific moment when you decided to actually look for something new?
7. How did you go about finding alternatives?
8. What options did you seriously consider?
9. Walk me through how you made the final decision.
10. What was migration day like?
11. How was the first month on the new software?

### Forces (15 min)
12. What was the most frustrating thing about your old software?
13. What excited you most about making a change?
14. What almost stopped you from switching?
15. What do you miss about the old software, if anything?

### Reflection (5 min)
16. Knowing what you know now, would you do anything differently?
17. What would you tell another business owner thinking about switching?
18. Is there anything about this experience I should have asked about?

### Close
19. Thank you so much. Would you be open to a brief follow-up if we have questions?
20. Do you know other business owners who've recently switched software?

## Probing Reminders
- "Tell me more about that"
- "What happened next?"
- "How did that feel?"
- [Use silence — wait 5 seconds after answers]
```



## Quality Tests

### Evidence Test
- Every finding cites specific data, quotes, or observations
- **PASS:** Each insight traces back to a named source or data point
- **FAIL:** Findings use vague language like 'it seems' or 'generally' without evidence

### Completeness Test
- All relevant dimensions of the framework have been considered
- **PASS:** Analysis addresses each framework dimension, noting gaps where data is unavailable
- **FAIL:** Key dimensions skipped without acknowledgment

### Actionability Test
- Recommendations are specific enough to act on immediately
- **PASS:** Each recommendation names who should do what by when
- **FAIL:** Recommendations are vague ('improve communication') without concrete actions

## Guidelines

1. **One topic per question** — compound questions confuse participants
2. **Open before closed** — "Tell me about..." before "Did you..."
3. **Past tense, specific events** — "When you did X" not "When you do X"
4. **Avoid leading questions** — "How was it?" not "That must have been hard?"
5. **Prepare but don't read** — know the guide, have a conversation
6. **Leave white space** — 60% them talking, 40% your questions
7. **Test the guide** — pilot with 1-2 participants, iterate

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Better Approach |
|--------------|---------|-----------------|
| Yes/no questions | Binary answers lack richness | Ask "how" and "what" |
| Double-barreled questions | Two questions = half answer | One question at a time |
| Hypotheticals | Imagined futures unreliable | Focus on real past events |
| Jargon | Participants mirror your terms | Use their language |
| Opinion questions | Opinions ≠ behavior | Ask what they did, not think |
| Interrupting | Kills depth | Wait for natural pauses |
| Filling silence | Participant may have more | Count to 5 before speaking |
