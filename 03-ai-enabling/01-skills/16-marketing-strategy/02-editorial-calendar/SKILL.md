# Editorial Calendar

An editorial calendar is not a spreadsheet full of dates. It is a production system that turns strategic intent into consistent, published content. The best calendars balance ambition with sustainability, because the only calendar that works is the one your team can actually execute against, week after week, without burning out.

Think of your calendar as a rhythm, not a deadline machine. Great publishing cadence has a heartbeat: predictable enough that your audience expects it, flexible enough that your team can maintain quality.

---

## Framework

### Calendar Architecture: Four Layers

Build your calendar from the top down. Each layer adds specificity.

| Layer | Timeframe | Purpose | Review Cycle |
|-------|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Annual Themes** | 12 months | Strategic direction, seasonal alignment | Set once, review quarterly |
| **Quarterly Focus** | 3 months | Priority topics, campaigns, launches | Set quarterly, review monthly |
| **Monthly Topics** | 4-5 weeks | Specific content topics and assignments | Set monthly, review weekly |
| **Weekly Execution** | 7 days | Production status, publish dates, promotion | Managed daily |

### Annual Theme Planning

Map 3-4 overarching themes across the year. Align with business cycles and audience needs.

| Quarter | Business Context | Theme Example | Content Angle |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|--------------|
| Q1 | Planning season, new budgets | "Foundations" | Strategy frameworks, planning guides |
| Q2 | Execution ramp-up | "Acceleration" | Tactics, optimization, case studies |
| Q3 | Mid-year assessment | "Refinement" | Data analysis, pivots, advanced techniques |
| Q4 | Budget defense, planning ahead | "Results & Vision" | ROI reports, predictions, year-in-review |

### Sustainable Cadence Calculation

Use this formula to set realistic publishing frequency:

| Factor | Assessment | Score |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| **Team size** (dedicated content creators) | 1 person = 1pt, 2-3 = 2pts, 4+ = 3pts | _/3 |
| **Content complexity** (research depth required) | Light = 3pts, Medium = 2pts, Heavy = 1pt | _/3 |
| **Approval layers** (review/sign-off steps) | 1 layer = 3pts, 2 = 2pts, 3+ = 1pt | _/3 |
| **Format variety** (number of formats produced) | 1 format = 3pts, 2-3 = 2pts, 4+ = 1pt | _/3 |

**Cadence recommendation by total score:**

| Score | Recommended Cadence |
|-------|-------------------|
| 10-12 | 3-5x per week |
| 7-9 | 2-3x per week |
| 5-6 | 1x per week |
| 3-4 | 2x per month |

**Critical rule:** Set cadence at 70% of your calculated maximum. The remaining 30% is your quality buffer and prevents burnout.

### Content Pipeline Stages

Every piece of content moves through these stages. Track each piece's current stage.

| Stage | Owner | Definition of Done | Typical Duration |
|-------|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Ideation** | Strategist | Topic approved, angle defined, brief written | Ongoing intake |
| **Draft** | Creator | First draft complete, matches brief | Varies by format |
| **Review** | Editor/Stakeholder | Feedback given, revisions requested | 1-2 review cycles |
| **Final** | Editor | Approved for publication, formatted | Ready to schedule |
| **Scheduled** | Publisher | Loaded into CMS/platform, publish date set | In queue |
| **Published** | Publisher | Live and accessible to audience | Published |
| **Promoted** | Distribution lead | Shared across distribution channels | Active promotion window |
| **Analyzed** | Analyst | Performance data collected, insights noted | Post-promotion |

### Pipeline Health Metrics

| Metric | Healthy Range | Warning Sign |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **Ideation backlog** | 2-4 weeks of approved ideas | Below 1 week or above 8 weeks |
| **Drafts in progress** | 1-2 per creator | 0 (stalled) or 4+ (overloaded) |
| **Review bottleneck** | Items in review less than 3 days | Items stuck in review over 5 days |
| **Buffer content** | 2+ weeks of scheduled content | Less than 1 week ahead |
| **Publish consistency** | Within 1 day of scheduled date | Missed dates more than 2x per month |

## Define Done

- [ ] Strategy grounded in audience data and market context
- [ ] Channels and tactics aligned to audience behavior
- [ ] Success metrics defined with specific targets
- [ ] Budget and resource requirements estimated

## Application

### Theme-to-Pillar Mapping

Map content pillars to calendar themes to ensure balanced coverage.

| | Pillar A | Pillar B | Pillar C | Pillar D |
|---|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Q1 Theme** | 2 pieces | 1 piece | 2 pieces | 1 piece |
| **Q2 Theme** | 1 piece | 2 pieces | 1 piece | 2 pieces |
| **Q3 Theme** | 2 pieces | 1 piece | 1 piece | 2 pieces |
| **Q4 Theme** | 1 piece | 2 pieces | 2 pieces | 1 piece |

**Balance rule:** No single pillar should exceed 40% of total content in any quarter. No pillar should drop below 15%.

### Batch Creation Strategy

Batch similar tasks to maximize creative flow and reduce context-switching.

| Batch Type | What to Group | Frequency |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **Ideation batch** | Brainstorm and brief 4-6 topics at once | Monthly |
| **Writing batch** | Draft 2-3 pieces in a focused session | Weekly or bi-weekly |
| **Visual batch** | Create graphics/thumbnails for multiple pieces | Weekly |
| **Review batch** | Editor reviews 3-5 pieces in one session | Weekly |
| **Scheduling batch** | Schedule and set up promotion for multiple pieces | Weekly |

### Buffer Content Library

Maintain a library of evergreen content that can fill gaps without feeling stale.

| Buffer Type | Purpose | Quantity to Maintain |
|-------------|---------|---------------------|
| **Evergreen articles** | Fill missed production weeks | 3-4 ready to publish |
| **Updated classics** | Refresh high-performing old content | 2-3 queued for refresh |
| **Curated roundups** | Quick-to-produce value pieces | Templates ready, 1-2 drafted |
| **Behind-the-scenes** | Authentic, low-production-cost content | 2-3 in reserve |

### Calendar Review Rhythm

| Review | Frequency | Participants | Focus |
|--------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Daily standup** | Daily (5 min) | Production team | What is publishing today? Any blockers? |
| **Weekly planning** | Weekly (30 min) | Content team | Next week's lineup, pipeline status |
| **Monthly retrospective** | Monthly (60 min) | Content + strategy | What worked, what didn't, adjust next month |
| **Quarterly strategy** | Quarterly (90 min) | Content + leadership | Theme review, pillar balance, cadence check |

## Quick Reference

### Editorial Calendar Setup Checklist

1. Define 3-4 annual themes aligned with business goals
2. Map content pillars to ensure quarterly balance
3. Calculate sustainable cadence using the scoring formula
4. Set cadence at 70% of maximum capacity
5. Establish pipeline stages and assign owners for each
6. Build a buffer library of 3-4 evergreen pieces
7. Create batch production schedule for efficiency
8. Set up review rhythm (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly)
9. Choose a calendar tool the whole team will actually use
10. Run the calendar for 30 days before making structural changes

### Emergency Calendar Decisions

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Creator out sick, content due | Pull from buffer library |
| Breaking industry news | Bump scheduled content, publish timely piece |
| Content underperforming 3 weeks straight | Monthly retrospective moved up, diagnose root cause |
| New product launch announced | Create dedicated mini-calendar for launch window |
| Team consistently missing deadlines | Reduce cadence immediately, assess pipeline bottlenecks |
| Seasonal opportunity (holiday, event) | Plan 6 weeks in advance, add to quarterly focus |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | What to Do Instead |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Calendar without strategy** | Dates without direction produce random content | Build strategy canvas first, calendar second |
| **100% capacity scheduling** | Zero margin means one sick day derails the month | Schedule at 70% capacity, build buffer |
| **Topic-first planning** | "We should write about X" without audience connection | Start with audience need, derive topics |
| **Rigid quarterly plans** | Markets shift, rigid plans produce irrelevant content | Set themes, keep specific topics flexible |
| **Ignoring pipeline bottlenecks** | Review stage is where most calendars die | Track pipeline health metrics weekly |
| **No buffer content** | One missed deadline creates a cascade of missed dates | Maintain 2+ weeks of ready-to-publish buffer |
| **Calendar lives in one person's head** | Single point of failure, no team visibility | Shared tool, visible to everyone, updated daily |
| **Skipping the retrospective** | Same mistakes repeated month after month | Monthly review is non-negotiable |


## Quality Tests

### Audience Alignment Test
- Strategy targets where the audience actually is, not assumptions
- **PASS:** Channel selection backed by audience research or data
- **FAIL:** Channels chosen based on trends rather than audience behavior

### Measurability Test
- Every tactic has a specific, measurable success metric
- **PASS:** KPIs defined with target numbers and measurement method
- **FAIL:** Goals are vague ('increase awareness') without measurable targets
