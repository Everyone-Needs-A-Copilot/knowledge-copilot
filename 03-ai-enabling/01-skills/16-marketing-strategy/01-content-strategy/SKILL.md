# Content Strategy

Great content strategy is not about producing more content. It is about producing the right content for the right people, delivered through the right channels, at the right cadence. Every piece you publish either builds compounding value or adds to the noise. Your job is to make sure it compounds.

The best content strategies pass one brutal test: "Would I subscribe to this?" If the answer is no, the strategy needs work. Content-market fit is just as real as product-market fit, and just as hard to achieve.

---

## Framework

### Content Strategy Canvas

Work through each layer sequentially. Each layer constrains and informs the next.

| Layer | Core Question | Output |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| **Audience** | Who specifically are we trying to reach? | 2-3 defined audience personas |
| **Message** | What do they need to hear from us? | Core narrative + supporting themes |
| **Format** | What format best delivers that message? | Prioritized format list |
| **Channel** | Where does this audience already congregate? | Channel priority matrix |
| **Cadence** | What rhythm can we sustain at quality? | Publishing schedule |
| **Measurement** | How do we know it is working? | KPI dashboard |

### Audience Persona Development

For each persona, complete this profile:

| Dimension | What to Capture | Example |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| **Role** | Job title, responsibilities, decision authority | VP of Marketing, owns budget |
| **Pain Points** | Top 3 frustrations they face daily | "Can't prove ROI to the board" |
| **Aspirations** | What success looks like to them | Recognized as growth driver |
| **Watering Holes** | Where they consume content already | LinkedIn, specific podcasts, Slack groups |
| **Content Preferences** | Format, length, depth, tone | Data-heavy, scannable, no fluff |
| **Trigger Events** | What prompts them to search for solutions | Budget planning season, missed targets |
| **Objections** | Why they might ignore your content | "We've tried that, it didn't work" |

### Content-Market Fit Assessment

Score each dimension 1-5:

| Dimension | Score | Evidence Required |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| **Audience clarity** | _/5 | Can you name 10 real people who match the persona? |
| **Problem specificity** | _/5 | Does your content address problems they actively search for? |
| **Differentiation** | _/5 | Can readers get this perspective elsewhere? |
| **Format fit** | _/5 | Does the format match how they consume content? |
| **Channel fit** | _/5 | Are you present where they already spend time? |
| **Cadence sustainability** | _/5 | Can you maintain this quality at this pace for 12 months? |

**Scoring:** 25-30 = strong fit, 18-24 = needs refinement, below 18 = rethink fundamentals.

### Distribution Channel Selection

| Channel Type | Examples | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-------------|----------|-----------|------------|
| **Owned** | Blog, newsletter, podcast, YouTube | Full control, compounding asset | Slow to build, requires consistency |
| **Earned** | PR, guest posts, shares, mentions | High trust, extended reach | Unpredictable, hard to scale |
| **Paid** | Ads, sponsorships, promoted content | Fast reach, targetable | Stops when budget stops |
| **Borrowed** | Social platforms, communities, forums | Built-in audience, discovery | Algorithm dependent, no ownership |

**Channel prioritization rule:** Invest 70% in owned channels (they compound), 20% in earned (they validate), 10% in paid (they accelerate). Borrowed channels are distribution tools, not foundations.

## Define Done

- [ ] Strategy grounded in audience data and market context
- [ ] Channels and tactics aligned to audience behavior
- [ ] Success metrics defined with specific targets
- [ ] Budget and resource requirements estimated

## Application

### Content Differentiation Strategy

Answer these five questions to find your differentiation angle:

1. **Proprietary data** -- Do you have access to data nobody else does? (customer data, industry surveys, internal benchmarks)
2. **Unique perspective** -- Do you hold a contrarian or uncommon view backed by experience?
3. **Practitioner credibility** -- Are you doing the thing you are writing about, not just observing it?
4. **Audience access** -- Can you bring voices and stories nobody else can surface?
5. **Format innovation** -- Can you deliver insights in a format competitors are not using?

If you cannot answer "yes" to at least two of these, your content will blend into the background.

### Strategy Validation Checklist

Before launching or revising a content strategy, validate against these criteria:

| Criterion | Pass/Fail | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Audience personas based on real conversations, not assumptions | | |
| Core narrative distinct from top 3 competitors | | |
| Format chosen based on audience preference, not team preference | | |
| Primary channel is where audience already spends time | | |
| Cadence is 70% of max capacity (leaves room for quality) | | |
| Measurement tied to business outcomes, not vanity metrics | | |
| Strategy can survive losing one team member | | |
| "Would I subscribe to this?" test passed by someone outside the team | | |

### The Subscription Test

Ask five people who match your target persona:

> "If this content showed up in your inbox every [cadence], would you open it? Would you forward it to a colleague?"

- If fewer than 3 say yes, revisit your message and differentiation.
- If they say "maybe" or "depends," your value proposition is not sharp enough.
- If they say "I already get something like this from [competitor]," you have a differentiation problem.

## Quick Reference

### Content Strategy in 10 Steps

1. Interview 5-10 real members of your target audience
2. Identify the 3 biggest pain points they share
3. Define your unique angle on those pain points
4. Choose 1-2 formats that match their consumption habits
5. Select 1 primary owned channel and 1-2 distribution channels
6. Set a cadence you can sustain for 12 months at quality
7. Create 4 weeks of content before launching
8. Publish consistently for 90 days before evaluating
9. Measure engagement depth (not just views) at 90-day mark
10. Adjust strategy based on what audience actually responds to

### Decision Table: When to Revisit Strategy

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Engagement declining 3 months straight | Audit content-market fit |
| Audience feedback mismatches intent | Revisit persona assumptions |
| Team burning out on cadence | Reduce frequency, increase quality |
| Competitor launched similar content | Sharpen differentiation |
| Business goals shifted | Realign strategy canvas top to bottom |
| New channel gaining audience traction | Evaluate channel addition |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | What to Do Instead |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **"More content = more results"** | Quantity without quality trains audience to ignore you | Publish less, make each piece count |
| **Copying competitor strategy** | Their audience is not your audience, their strengths are not yours | Find your own differentiation angle |
| **Starting with format** | "We need a podcast" before knowing if the audience wants one | Start with audience, let format follow |
| **Skipping distribution planning** | Great content with no distribution plan is a tree falling in an empty forest | Spend 50% of effort on distribution |
| **Vanity metric obsession** | Page views and follower counts do not pay bills | Track metrics that connect to business outcomes |
| **Strategy by committee** | Consensus produces bland, undifferentiated content | One clear editorial voice and vision |
| **Ignoring the subscription test** | If you would not consume it, neither will they | Be honest about whether the content is genuinely valuable |
| **Launching without a buffer** | Missing one week kills momentum and credibility | Build 4 weeks of buffer before going live |


## Quality Tests

### Audience Alignment Test
- Strategy targets where the audience actually is, not assumptions
- **PASS:** Channel selection backed by audience research or data
- **FAIL:** Channels chosen based on trends rather than audience behavior

### Measurability Test
- Every tactic has a specific, measurable success metric
- **PASS:** KPIs defined with target numbers and measurement method
- **FAIL:** Goals are vague ('increase awareness') without measurable targets
