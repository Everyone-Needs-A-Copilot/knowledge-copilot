# Business Finance

Business finance for solopreneurs and small service businesses is fundamentally different from personal finance. Personal finance optimizes for security and lifestyle. Business finance optimizes for sustainability, growth capacity, and value creation. Confusing the two -- treating business revenue as personal income or ignoring business metrics -- creates blind spots that can turn a profitable-seeming business into a fragile one.

The core discipline is maintaining clear separation between business and personal finances, tracking the metrics that predict business health (not just trailing revenue), and making decisions based on margins rather than top-line revenue. A business doing $300K in revenue with 15% margins is less healthy than one doing $200K with 40% margins, even though the first "makes more money."

## Framework

### Business Health Dashboard

**Primary Metrics (track monthly)**

| Metric | Formula | Healthy Range | Warning Sign |
|--------|---------|---------------|-------------|
| Gross revenue | Total invoiced/collected | Trending up or stable | Declining 3+ consecutive months |
| Cost of delivery | Direct costs to deliver services | < 30% of revenue (services) | Growing faster than revenue |
| Gross margin | (Revenue - Cost of Delivery) / Revenue | > 60% for services | Below 50% |
| Operating expenses | All business costs except delivery | < 25% of revenue | Growing without revenue growth |
| Net margin | (Revenue - All Expenses) / Revenue | > 25% for solo services | Below 15% |
| Owner's compensation | Salary + distributions taken | Sustainable and defensible | Exceeds net profit consistently |
| Business cash reserve | Cash available for business operations | 2-3 months of operating expenses | Less than 1 month |

**Secondary Metrics (track quarterly)**

| Metric | Formula | Purpose |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Revenue per client | Total revenue / active clients | Measures client value and pricing health |
| Client acquisition cost | Marketing + sales effort / new clients | Measures growth efficiency |
| Client lifetime value | Average revenue per client x average relationship length | Informs acceptable acquisition cost |
| Revenue concentration | Largest client revenue / total revenue | Measures dependency risk |
| Effective hourly rate | Net income / hours worked | True measure of time value |
| Pipeline coverage | Pipeline value / quarterly revenue target | Predicts future revenue |

### Revenue Analysis Framework

**Revenue Source Categorization:**

| Source Type | Characteristics | Risk Profile |
|------------|----------------|-------------|
| Recurring retainers | Predictable, contractual, monthly | Low (until contract ends) |
| Project-based | Variable, defined scope and timeline | Medium (feast-or-famine risk) |
| Hourly/time-based | Variable, tied to available hours | Medium (capacity-capped) |
| Product/passive | Decoupled from time, scalable | Low-Medium (but requires upfront investment) |
| One-time/ad hoc | Unpredictable, opportunistic | High (cannot plan around it) |

**Revenue Health Assessment:**

| Factor | Healthy | Concerning | Critical |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| Recurring % of total revenue | > 50% | 25-50% | < 25% |
| Revenue trend (3-month) | Growing or stable | Flat | Declining |
| Pipeline coverage (next quarter) | > 2x | 1-2x | < 1x |
| Client concentration (largest client) | < 20% of revenue | 20-40% | > 40% |
| Number of revenue sources | 3+ types | 2 types | 1 type |

### Expense Management Framework

**Expense Categories for Service Businesses:**

| Category | Examples | Target % of Revenue |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| Cost of delivery | Subcontractors, project-specific tools, client-facing costs | 15-30% |
| Operating overhead | Rent, utilities, insurance, general software | 5-10% |
| Marketing and sales | Advertising, content creation, sales tools | 5-15% |
| Professional services | Accounting, legal, consulting | 2-5% |
| Technology | SaaS, hardware, infrastructure | 3-8% |
| Professional development | Training, conferences, certifications | 2-5% |
| Owner compensation | Salary, benefits, retirement | 40-60% |

**Expense Efficiency Ratios:**

| Ratio | Calculation | Target |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| Revenue per dollar of expense | Revenue / total expenses | > 1.3x (30%+ net margin) |
| Marketing efficiency | Revenue from marketing / marketing spend | > 5x |
| Technology ROI | Revenue / technology spend | > 15x |
| Overhead ratio | Overhead / revenue | < 15% |

### Profitability Analysis

**Service-Level Profitability:**

For each service offering, calculate:

| Component | Calculation |
|-----------|-------------|
| Service revenue | Total revenue from this service |
| Direct costs | Costs directly attributable to delivering this service |
| Allocated overhead | Share of overhead (usually by revenue proportion or time) |
| Service profit | Revenue - direct costs - allocated overhead |
| Service margin | Service profit / service revenue |

**Service Profitability Decision Matrix:**

| Margin | Volume | Action |
|--------|--------|--------|
| High (> 40%) | High | Core offering -- protect and grow |
| High (> 40%) | Low | Premium offering -- market more aggressively |
| Medium (20-40%) | High | Workhorse -- optimize for efficiency |
| Medium (20-40%) | Low | Evaluate -- worth keeping if strategic |
| Low (< 20%) | High | Efficiency problem -- reduce costs or raise prices |
| Low (< 20%) | Low | Candidate for elimination or redesign |

### Business Cash Flow (Distinct from Personal)

**Business Cash Flow Components:**

| Inflow | Outflow |
|--------|---------|
| Client payments received | Contractor/vendor payments |
| Product sales | Software and tool subscriptions |
| Interest/investment income | Insurance premiums |
| Tax refunds (business) | Marketing and advertising |
| | Professional services fees |
| | Tax payments (estimated, payroll) |
| | Owner distributions/salary |
| | Equipment purchases |

**Cash Flow Timing Risks:**

| Risk | Description | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Payment lag | Clients pay 30-60 days after invoice | Shorten payment terms, offer early payment discount |
| Seasonal dip | Predictable low-revenue periods | Build reserve during peak, reduce discretionary in dip |
| Large expense timing | Annual insurance, quarterly taxes, equipment | Amortize monthly into a sinking fund |
| Client concentration | Losing one large client creates immediate shortfall | Diversify client base, maintain 3-month reserve |
| Scope creep | Delivering more than scoped without additional billing | Define scope clearly, track and bill overages |

### Client/Revenue Concentration Risk

| Concentration Level | Largest Client % | Risk Assessment | Action |
|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|
| Diversified | < 15% | Low | Maintain current mix |
| Moderate | 15-25% | Acceptable | Monitor, actively pursue new clients |
| Concentrated | 25-40% | Elevated | Priority: diversify, build pipeline |
| Dependent | 40-60% | High | Urgent: acquire new clients, reduce scope if possible |
| Critical | > 60% | Very High | This is employment, not a business; restructure immediately |

**Top-3 Client Test:**
If losing your top 3 clients simultaneously would make the business unviable, concentration risk is too high regardless of individual client percentages.

### Quarterly Business Health Check

In addition to monthly review, quarterly check:

- [ ] Service-level profitability analysis (which services are most/least profitable?)
- [ ] Client concentration risk assessment (has it improved or worsened?)
- [ ] Pricing review (are effective hourly rates meeting targets?)
- [ ] Expense audit (any subscriptions or costs that can be eliminated?)
- [ ] Pipeline review (is pipeline building or depleting?)
- [ ] Competitive positioning (any market changes affecting business?)
- [ ] Annual projection update (on track for annual targets?)

### Break-Even Analysis

```
Monthly break-even = Fixed costs / (1 - variable cost ratio)

Where:
  Fixed costs = rent + insurance + subscriptions + owner base salary
  Variable cost ratio = variable costs / revenue

Example:
  Fixed costs: $5,000/month
  Variable cost ratio: 0.25 (25% of revenue goes to direct delivery costs)
  Break-even: $5,000 / (1 - 0.25) = $6,667/month in revenue
```

## Quick Reference

### Key Business Metrics Cheat Sheet

| Metric | Quick Formula | Healthy Target (Services) |
|--------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Gross margin | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | > 60% |
| Net margin | Net profit / Revenue | > 25% |
| Operating expense ratio | OpEx / Revenue | < 25% |
| Client concentration | Top client / Total revenue | < 25% |
| Revenue per client | Revenue / Clients | Trending up |
| Effective hourly rate | Net income / Hours worked | Above target |
| Cash reserve | Business cash / Monthly expenses | 2-3 months |
| Pipeline coverage | Pipeline / Quarterly target | > 2x |

### Monthly Review Checklist

- [ ] Record all revenue by source and client
- [ ] Record all expenses by category
- [ ] Calculate gross and net margins
- [ ] Compare to prior month and 3-month average
- [ ] Check cash reserve level
- [ ] Review client concentration
- [ ] Update pipeline status
- [ ] Identify top 3 action items

### Red Flags Requiring Immediate Action

| Red Flag | Implication | Response |
|----------|------------|----------|
| Net margin below 10% for 3 months | Business is not sustainable | Raise prices, cut costs, or both |
| Single client > 50% of revenue | Business failure if client leaves | Priority: diversify immediately |
| Cash reserve below 1 month | One bad month = crisis | Reduce all discretionary spending |
| Revenue declining 3+ months | Demand problem or market shift | Diagnose cause: pipeline, pricing, or market |
| Effective hourly rate below minimum wage | Business is exploiting the owner | Fundamental restructure needed |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Treating all revenue as personal income | Ignores taxes, business expenses, reinvestment needs | Maintain separate business and personal accounts |
| Tracking only revenue, not margins | High revenue with low margins is a job, not a business | Track net margin as the primary success metric |
| Ignoring client concentration | Creates existential risk from a single client decision | Monitor concentration quarterly, diversify actively |
| Not knowing effective hourly rate | Cannot evaluate whether business is worth running | Calculate monthly: net income / hours worked |
| Cutting marketing when revenue dips | Accelerates the decline; marketing drives future revenue | Maintain or increase marketing during dips |
| No separate business cash reserve | Personal emergency fund is not business working capital | Maintain 2-3 months of business expenses in a business account |
| Pricing based on competitors rather than costs | May price below profitability | Know your costs and margin targets first, then position competitively |
| Measuring success by revenue alone | A $500K business with 5% margin is less successful than $200K at 40% | Profit and margin are the measures that matter |
| Not reviewing financials monthly | Problems compound unnoticed; small issues become big ones | Monthly review is non-negotiable for business owners |


## Quality Tests

### Accuracy Test
- All calculations are correct and assumptions are stated
- **PASS:** Numbers are verifiable and assumptions clearly documented
- **FAIL:** Rough estimates presented as precise figures

### Completeness Test
- Analysis considers tax implications and opportunity costs
- **PASS:** Tax impact calculated for each recommendation
- **FAIL:** Pre-tax returns presented without noting tax drag
