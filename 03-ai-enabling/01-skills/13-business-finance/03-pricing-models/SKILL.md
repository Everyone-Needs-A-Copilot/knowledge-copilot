# Pricing Models

Pricing is the single highest-leverage decision in a service business. A 10% price increase on $200K in revenue adds $20K to the top line with zero additional cost -- going directly to profit. By contrast, winning $20K in additional revenue at the same price requires finding new clients, delivering more work, and absorbing incremental costs. Yet most service businesses spend far more energy on sales than on pricing.

The core mistake is pricing based on time and cost rather than value. Cost-plus pricing (calculate your costs, add a margin) ensures you do not lose money but leaves value on the table. Value-based pricing (price based on the outcome delivered to the client) captures a share of the value you create. The right model depends on your business, your clients, and the nature of the engagement -- but understanding all models enables deliberate choice rather than default behavior.

## Framework

### Pricing Model Comparison

| Model | How It Works | Best For | Risk |
|-------|-------------|----------|------|
| Hourly | Bill per hour worked | Exploration, unclear scope, early relationships | Income capped by hours, penalizes efficiency |
| Daily/Half-Day | Bill per day or half-day | Workshops, on-site work, short engagements | Less granular than hourly, more than project |
| Project/Fixed | Quote a fixed price for defined deliverables | Well-defined scope, experienced providers | Scope creep can destroy margin |
| Retainer | Fixed monthly fee for ongoing access/services | Long-term relationships, recurring needs | Can become unprofitable if scope undefined |
| Value-Based | Price based on expected client outcome/ROI | High-impact engagements, quantifiable outcomes | Requires understanding client's economics |
| Performance | Base fee + bonus tied to results | Aligned incentives, measurable outcomes | Risk if outcomes are outside your control |
| Productized | Standardized scope and price, sold as a "product" | Repeatable services, scalability | Less flexibility, commoditization risk |

### Effective Hourly Rate Calculation

Regardless of which model you use externally, always calculate your effective hourly rate to evaluate profitability:

```
Effective Hourly Rate = Net Revenue from Engagement / Total Hours Spent

Where total hours includes:
  - Direct delivery hours
  - Project management hours
  - Communication and meetings
  - Revision and rework hours
  - Administrative hours related to the engagement
```

**Effective Rate Benchmarks:**

| Rate | Assessment | Action |
|------|-----------|--------|
| > 3x your hourly rate | Excellent -- value pricing is working | Maintain or test higher prices |
| 2-3x your hourly rate | Good -- healthy margin above time cost | Optimize for efficiency to push higher |
| 1-2x your hourly rate | Adequate -- covers costs with modest margin | Look for pricing or efficiency improvements |
| 1x your hourly rate | Break-even on time | Not sustainable; reprice or stop offering |
| < 1x your hourly rate | Losing money on time value | Immediately address: reprice, rescope, or drop |

### Margin Target Setting

**Target Margin by Pricing Model:**

| Model | Gross Margin Target | Why |
|-------|-------------------|-----|
| Hourly | 50-60% | Limited upside; margin comes from rate level |
| Project | 40-60% | Must buffer for scope variance; higher margin compensates for risk |
| Retainer | 50-70% | Predictable; margin should be high since you bear availability risk |
| Value-Based | 60-80% | You are capturing value, not billing costs |
| Productized | 70-85% | Repeatable delivery should drive costs down |

**Margin Calculation:**

```
Gross Margin = (Revenue - Direct Costs) / Revenue x 100

Direct Costs include:
  - Your time (at your internal hourly cost)
  - Subcontractor costs
  - Software/tools specific to engagement
  - Travel costs
  - Any other costs directly attributable to the engagement
```

**Internal Hourly Cost (what your time actually costs):**

```
Internal Hourly Cost = (Annual Salary Target + Benefits + Overhead Allocation) / Billable Hours Per Year

Example:
  Salary target: $120,000
  Benefits (health, retirement): $20,000
  Overhead allocation: $15,000
  Billable hours per year: 1,400 (accounting for vacation, admin, sales)
  Internal hourly cost: $155,000 / 1,400 = $110.71/hour
```

### Pricing Scenario Modeling

**Scenario 1: Rate Change Impact**

| Variable | Current | +10% Rate | +20% Rate | -10% Rate |
|----------|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Hourly rate | $200 | $220 | $240 | $180 |
| Hours per month | 120 | 120 | 120 | 120 |
| Monthly revenue | $24,000 | $26,400 | $28,800 | $21,600 |
| Annual revenue impact | -- | +$28,800 | +$57,600 | -$28,800 |
| Clients you can lose and break even | -- | 1 in 11 | 1 in 6 | N/A |

The "clients you can lose" calculation is critical for price increase decisions: if you raise rates 10%, you can lose approximately 9% of clients and still make the same revenue -- while working less.

**Scenario 2: Model Shift Impact**

| Metric | Hourly @ $200/hr | Project @ $15K/project | Retainer @ $5K/mo |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Revenue per engagement | $200 x hours | $15,000 fixed | $5,000/month |
| Hours per engagement | Variable | ~60 estimated | ~30/month |
| Effective rate | $200 | $250 (if 60hrs) | $167 (if 30hrs) |
| Revenue predictability | Low | Medium | High |
| Scope risk | Low (client's risk) | High (your risk) | Medium (defined retainer scope) |
| Client budget certainty | Low (they do not know total) | High (they know the price) | High (fixed monthly) |

**Scenario 3: Volume vs Rate Trade-off**

| Approach | Rate | Clients Needed for $200K | Hours/Month | Sustainability |
|----------|------|--------------------------|-------------|---------------|
| High rate, fewer clients | $300/hr | 4-5 clients | 56 hrs | High -- more time margin |
| Medium rate, medium clients | $200/hr | 7-8 clients | 84 hrs | Medium -- balanced |
| Low rate, many clients | $150/hr | 10-12 clients | 112 hrs | Low -- approaching burnout |

### Price Increase Strategy

**When to Raise Prices:**

| Signal | Interpretation | Action |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| Fully booked with waitlist | Demand exceeds supply | Raise prices 10-20% |
| No price increase in 12+ months | Inflation alone erodes value | Minimum inflation adjustment |
| New capabilities or credentials | Increased value proposition | Raise prices to reflect new value |
| All proposals accepted | Prices are too low | Raise until 70-80% acceptance rate |
| Effective hourly rate declining | Scope or efficiency problem | Raise prices or restructure engagements |

**How to Implement Price Increases:**

| Method | How It Works | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| New clients only | New rate for new engagements, existing clients keep current rate | Low-risk, gradual transition |
| Contract renewal | Increase at natural renewal points | Retainer clients, annual contracts |
| Grandfathering with sunset | Existing clients keep old rate for X months, then transition | Respectful transition with defined endpoint |
| Across the board | All clients move to new rate on a specific date | When you need immediate impact |
| Tiered introduction | Introduce a new premium tier above current pricing | Test demand for higher-value offering |

**Price Increase Communication Framework:**

1. Lead with value delivered (not your cost increases)
2. Provide advance notice (30-90 days)
3. State the new rate clearly
4. Offer to discuss if they have questions
5. Do not apologize or over-explain

### Cost-Plus vs Value-Based Decision Framework

| Factor | Favors Cost-Plus | Favors Value-Based |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Scope clarity | Unclear, exploratory | Well-defined outcome |
| Client sophistication | Price-sensitive, comparison-shoppers | Outcome-focused, ROI-aware |
| Your expertise level | Building experience | Deep expertise, proven results |
| Outcome measurability | Hard to quantify value | Clear, quantifiable value |
| Competitive landscape | Commoditized service, many alternatives | Differentiated, few can deliver |
| Risk tolerance | Prefer predictable income | Willing to bet on value delivery |
| Relationship stage | New client, building trust | Established relationship, trust exists |

### Value-Based Pricing Methodology

**Step 1: Quantify the client's problem**
- What is this problem costing them? (lost revenue, wasted time, missed opportunities)
- What is the value of solving it? (additional revenue, time saved, risk avoided)

**Step 2: Determine your share of value**
- Industry norm: capture 10-25% of value created
- Higher share if: you are the only one who can do it, outcome is certain
- Lower share if: outcome is uncertain, client contributes significantly to the result

**Step 3: Set the price**

```
Value-Based Price = Value to Client x Your Share Percentage

Example:
  Client problem: losing $500K/year due to inefficient process
  Your solution: redesign process, expected to save $300K/year
  Your share: 15% of first-year savings
  Price: $300,000 x 15% = $45,000

  Compare: if this took 100 hours at $200/hour = $20,000
  Value-based price captures $25,000 more = the value premium
```

**Step 4: Validate**
- Is the price defensible? (client gets $300K for $45K investment = 6.7x ROI)
- Would the client pay this? (test with a conversation, not a proposal)
- Can you deliver with high confidence? (if not, reduce price or add performance component)

## Application

### Pricing Audit Checklist

Run this audit annually or when revenue/profitability changes:

- [ ] Calculate effective hourly rate for each service offering
- [ ] Compare effective rate to internal hourly cost (must be higher)
- [ ] Calculate gross margin for each service offering
- [ ] Identify lowest-margin offerings (below 40%)
- [ ] Check proposal acceptance rate (target 70-80%)
- [ ] Review time since last price increase
- [ ] Compare rates to market (are you below market?)
- [ ] Calculate how much a 10% increase would add to annual revenue
- [ ] Identify services that could shift to value-based pricing
- [ ] Model pricing scenarios for proposed changes

### Retainer Structuring Guide

| Element | Definition | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Scope | What is included in the retainer | "Up to 30 hours of consulting and 2 deliverables per month" |
| Exclusions | What is not included (billed separately) | "Travel, third-party costs, scope beyond defined services" |
| Term | Duration of agreement | "12 months with 60-day cancellation notice" |
| Rollover policy | Do unused hours carry over? | "Hours do not roll over" (recommended) |
| Overage rate | Rate for work beyond retainer scope | "Hours beyond 30 billed at $X/hr" |
| Rate lock | Is the rate fixed for the term? | "Rate locked for 12-month term" |
| Payment terms | When and how payment is due | "Due 1st of month, net 5 days" |

## Quick Reference

### Pricing Model Selection Guide

| If you... | Use this model |
|-----------|---------------|
| Cannot estimate scope accurately | Hourly |
| Know exactly what to deliver and how long it takes | Project/Fixed |
| Provide ongoing support or advisory | Retainer |
| Can quantify the ROI for the client | Value-Based |
| Deliver the same thing repeatedly | Productized |
| Want aligned incentives with client | Performance |

### Price Increase Decision Tree

1. Has it been 12+ months since your last increase? **Yes = increase (at minimum, inflation).**
2. Is your acceptance rate > 90%? **Yes = you are underpriced. Increase 10-20%.**
3. Are you at capacity with a waitlist? **Yes = increase until demand normalizes.**
4. Has your expertise or credential improved? **Yes = increase to reflect new value.**
5. None of the above? **Evaluate margins. If below target, increase.**

### Key Pricing Formulas

| Formula | Calculation |
|---------|-------------|
| Effective hourly rate | Net revenue / total hours (including all related time) |
| Internal hourly cost | (Salary + benefits + overhead) / billable hours per year |
| Gross margin | (Revenue - direct costs) / revenue |
| Break-even rate | Total annual costs / billable hours per year |
| Value-based price | Value to client x 10-25% |
| Max client loss at X% increase | ~(X / (100 + X)) of clients |

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Pricing by "what feels right" | Ignores costs, market, and value; leaves money on the table | Calculate internal cost, research market, quantify value |
| Racing to the bottom on price | Attracts price-sensitive clients, destroys margin | Compete on value and expertise, not price |
| Hourly billing for everything | Penalizes your efficiency, caps income at hours x rate | Use project or value-based pricing where scope is clear |
| Not tracking effective hourly rate | Cannot know if engagements are actually profitable | Calculate for every engagement, compare to internal cost |
| Giving discounts to "land" clients | Sets expectations, rarely leads to full-price renewals | Offer reduced scope instead of reduced price |
| No scope definition on fixed-price work | Scope creep eats your margin to zero or negative | Define scope, deliverables, revisions, and exclusions in writing |
| Charging the same rate for 3+ years | Inflation erodes your real income 3-5% annually | Review and adjust prices annually at minimum |
| Pricing based on competitor's rates | You do not know their costs, margins, or strategy | Know your own costs, target your own margins |
| Apologizing for your prices | Signals you do not believe in your own value | State prices confidently; value justifies the number |
| Free "quick" work for existing clients | Accumulates into significant unbilled time | Track all time; bill or explicitly comp as relationship investment |
| Same price for all clients regardless of value | Undercharges high-value clients, overcharges low-value | Segment pricing by value delivered, not cost to serve |


## Quality Tests

### Accuracy Test
- All calculations are correct and assumptions are stated
- **PASS:** Numbers are verifiable and assumptions clearly documented
- **FAIL:** Rough estimates presented as precise figures

### Completeness Test
- Analysis considers tax implications and opportunity costs
- **PASS:** Tax impact calculated for each recommendation
- **FAIL:** Pre-tax returns presented without noting tax drag
